Got a letter from the water utility about a required test? Many Fort Myers water customers with an irrigation system, pool fill line, or certain commercial connections are required to have their backflow preventer tested annually. If a notice showed up in the mail, that's exactly what this service covers, and the paperwork gets filed with the utility on your behalf.

What Backflow Prevention Actually Does

Why This Exists

A backflow preventer stops contaminated water, like water sitting in an irrigation line or pool fill line, from siphoning backward into the clean municipal water supply during a pressure drop. Most residential irrigation systems and many commercial connections in Fort Myers are required by the local utility to have a tested, certified device, checked annually to confirm it's still functioning correctly. This isn't an optional upsell; it's a real compliance requirement tied to protecting the shared water supply, and failing to test on schedule can result in a utility notice or, eventually, service restrictions. The requirement applies whether the irrigation system was installed recently or has been in place for years, since the test confirms the device is still working, not just that it exists.

Who Typically Needs This

Common Situations

Homes With Irrigation Systems

Most residential sprinkler systems tie into a backflow preventer that needs annual testing under local utility rules.

Pool Fill Lines

A dedicated pool fill connection is another common point requiring a tested backflow device.

Commercial Properties

Many commercial water connections have backflow requirements tied to occupancy type and connection size.

New Construction Or Remodels

A new irrigation system or commercial connection typically needs a backflow device installed and tested before final approval.

Before You Call

What To Have Ready

  • Have any notice from your water utility on hand, since it usually specifies the deadline and device information needed.
  • Know roughly how many backflow devices are on the property, such as separate irrigation and pool fill connections.
  • For new construction, check with your contractor about whether a backflow device has already been installed and just needs testing.
  • If your device is located in an easily accessible spot in the yard, mention that when you call, since access can affect scheduling.
FAQs

Common Questions

Do I really need to test my backflow device every year?

If your property has an irrigation system, pool fill line, or certain commercial connections, most Fort Myers-area utilities require annual testing and certification.

Can I test the device myself?

No, testing requires a certified tester with calibrated equipment. A self-check isn't accepted by most utilities as valid certification.

What happens if I miss the testing deadline?

Utilities typically send a notice first, but continued non-compliance can eventually lead to service restrictions, so it's worth scheduling promptly once notified.

Does a new device need testing right after installation?

Yes, a new backflow device is typically tested and certified as part of the installation itself before it's considered compliant.

Do you handle the utility paperwork?

Yes, we file the required test certification with the utility on your behalf after testing is complete.

Do you install new backflow devices?

Yes, for new irrigation systems, pool connections, or commercial properties that don't yet have one installed.
