A City Of Its Own

Bonita Springs Is A Separate Market

Bonita Springs is an incorporated city of 53,644 residents, according to the 2020 U.S. Census, at the southern edge of Lee County, built around the Imperial River, Estero Bay, and a stretch of Gulf shoreline at Bonita Beach and Barefoot Beach. It sits well south of Fort Myers, so we treat it as its own dispatch zone. Before a truck leaves, we confirm the address, the community, and the utility, because Bonita Springs works differently from the rest of Lee County.

Most water and sewer service comes from Bonita Springs Utilities, a member-owned nonprofit founded in 1970. It blends water from a reverse osmosis plant and a lime-softening plant, and nearly all of its treated wastewater is reused for irrigation. That is why many communities water their lawns from a separate reclaimed line. A green, soggy yard here is not always a household leak, and a meter reading settles the question.

Three Housing Eras

What Is Actually In The Walls

The oldest housing sits in the grid of numbered streets around Old 41 Road and the historic downtown. Newer development spread along US 41 and inland, and most of the large gated communities arrived in the 1990s and later. ZIP-level housing data shows most homes in 34134 were built in the 1990s. Older Old Bonita properties are where we expect original galvanized supply lines and aging drain pipe, while the 1990s communities lean toward water heater replacements, slab leak diagnosis, and backflow testing for irrigation connections.

Beachfront and canal-side condos near Bonita Beach and Barefoot Beach add salt air, shared risers, and building shutoff valves to the picture. A leak in a shared riser can reach several units, so the first call should go to us and to the building manager. Parts of ZIP 34134 also cross the county line into Collier County, and we confirm which side a property sits on when we schedule. Repairs follow the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and condo associations may ask for proof of licensing before work starts.

If You Have A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak

Before We Arrive

  • Shut off the water at the main valve, or at the unit shutoff in a condo. If a shared riser is leaking, call building management right away as well.
  • Turn off power to wet rooms and stay clear of standing water near outlets.
  • Move rugs, electronics, and furniture away from the water.
  • Call our 24/7 line and give us the community or building name plus any gate or elevator access details.

Bonita Springs Plumbing Questions

Do you cover emergency plumbing in ZIP 34134?

Yes. ZIP 34134 includes the older downtown grid, Barefoot Beach, and the Spring Creek area, and we dispatch there around the clock.

Which utility serves my Bonita Springs address?

Most of the city is served by Bonita Springs Utilities, a nonprofit cooperative. Your water bill names your provider, and we can help you read it if you are unsure.

Why is my lawn wet if I do not have a leak?

Many communities here irrigate with reclaimed water from a separate system, so a wet lawn may be an irrigation issue and not a break in your home's water line. A quick meter check tells us which.

Do you handle beachfront condo and shared riser leaks?

Yes. We coordinate with the building manager, find which unit or riser is leaking, and explain the repair options before work starts.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Bonita Springs?

Yes. We cover 34134 and 34135, from the older streets near Old 41 Road and the beaches to the inland gated communities near The Brooks.

Is emergency service available in Bonita Springs?

Yes. The emergency line answers every hour of every day, and we dispatch to Bonita Springs at night, on weekends, and on holidays.

How fast can you respond to a Bonita Springs emergency?

Bonita Springs is one of the farthest areas we dispatch to from Fort Myers, so we give you an honest arrival window when you call and tell you if traffic changes it.
