Two Utilities, One Village

Why Estero Plumbing Starts With A Map

The Village of Estero incorporated on December 31, 2014 and sits in southern Lee County, with Florida Gulf Coast University on its edge and Southwest Florida International Airport less than five miles to the north. Plumbing here does not look like plumbing in older Fort Myers. Water comes from Lee County Utilities across much of the village, while Bonita Springs Utilities supplies properties south of Williams Road. Which utility serves your street decides who owns a problem at the meter, so we sort that out first on the phone.

Irrigation adds a second wrinkle. Bonita Springs Utilities sells its treated wastewater as reclaimed irrigation water, and master-planned communities such as The Brooks run their sprinklers from that separate system. A soggy yard in one of those communities is not automatically a leak in your drinking water line. A quick meter check tells us which system is losing water before anyone digs.

Housing Stock

A Newer Market With Its Own Failure Pattern

Census-based figures compiled by City-Data show ZIP 33928 growing from about 8,850 residents in 2000 to roughly 20,800 in 2010, with an estimate near 33,000 today. That growth means most Estero homes are slab-on-grade construction with plastic drain lines, so the cast iron collapses we see on older Fort Myers streets are rare here. The calls lean toward slab leaks in supply lines, water heaters tucked into garage closets, scale from hard water on fixtures, and irrigation connections that need annual backflow testing. Those backflow rules trace back to the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and the utility enforces them.

A few older streets near the Estero River and Koreshan State Park predate the master-planned boom. The 1910 U.S. Census counted only 299 residents in the area, and homes from that era or the decades after can still carry older supply and drain materials. We check the age of the pipe before we recommend a repair, because the right fix on a 1995 slab is not the right fix on a 1960s cottage.

If You Have A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak

Before We Arrive

  • Shut off the water at the main valve. In a condo or townhome, use the unit shutoff or ask the association where the building valve is.
  • Turn off power to any room with standing water, and stay out of that room if water is near outlets or appliances.
  • Clear the immediate area of rugs, electronics, and furniture so the floor can be dried quickly.
  • Call our 24/7 line and tell us the community name, since gate codes and visitor lists can slow a truck at the entrance.

Estero Plumbing Questions

Do you cover emergency plumbing in ZIP 33928?

Yes. ZIP 33928 is Estero's main residential ZIP, and we dispatch to its gated communities, condos, and single-family streets around the clock.

Which utility serves my Estero address?

Most of the village is on Lee County Utilities water, and properties south of Williams Road are served by Bonita Springs Utilities. Your water bill names your provider, and we can help you read it if you are unsure.

Is a wet lawn always a plumbing leak?

No. Some communities irrigate with reclaimed water from a separate system, so a soggy yard can be an irrigation problem instead of a leak in your home's water line. A meter check separates the two quickly.

Do you work inside gated communities and with HOAs?

Yes. Give us the community name and any gate instructions when you call, and we coordinate access with the gate or management office. Common-area lines are handled through our commercial service.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Estero?

Yes. We cover 33928 and 33967, from the golf communities south of Corkscrew Road to the northern edge near FGCU and the Fort Myers line.

Is emergency service available in Estero?

Yes. Our emergency line is open 24 hours a day, every day, and dispatch to Estero runs at night, on weekends, and on holidays.

How fast can you respond to an Estero emergency?

We dispatch from Fort Myers, so we give you a realistic arrival window on the first call instead of a promise. Traffic on US 41 and Interstate 75 affects the drive, and we tell you when it does.
