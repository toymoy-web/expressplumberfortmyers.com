How San Carlos Park's Streets Differ

Ben Hill Griffin Pkwy Corridor

Dense 1970s-80s residential development along this corridor runs a mix of galvanized steel and cast iron supply and drain lines, now well past their 30-to-50-year service window.

West Of Three Oaks Pkwy

Pockets here were built slightly later, in the 1980s and 1990s, and are more likely to carry early polybutylene, a plastic pipe material known for brittle failure at fittings as it ages.

Why The Housing Stock Matters Here

Three Pipe Materials, One ZIP Code

San Carlos Park's population grew fast through the 1970s, 1980s, and 1990s, producing a dense mix of single-family homes, townhomes, and multi-family units, all built within a few decades of each other but using three genuinely different pipe materials depending on exactly when a given street went in. That matters for diagnosis: a galvanized steel leak behaves differently than a polybutylene fitting failure, and guessing at the material before opening a wall wastes time on the wrong repair approach. Lee County's hard water adds a second layer, accelerating corrosion in the galvanized sections specifically, while the polybutylene sections fail more from age and UV exposure at exposed fittings than from mineral buildup. The area's higher flood risk during heavy rain adds a third factor, since saturated ground stresses drainage right when older pipe is already at its most vulnerable.
