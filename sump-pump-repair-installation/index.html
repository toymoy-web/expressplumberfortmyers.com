Honest Context For Fort Myers

Sump Pumps Are Rare Here, And That Is The Point

Sump pumps are not standard equipment in most Fort Myers homes. The water table is high, nearly every house sits on a slab, and basements are almost nonexistent, so most homeowners have never heard one run. Where a pump does exist, it matters a great deal. It might drain a low-lying lot, protect a ground-level garage or utility room, lift waste from a below-grade bathroom, empty a pool equipment pit, or keep a condo garage sump clear.

Local conditions work against these pumps. The rainy season from roughly June through October can fill a pit in minutes. Fine sand carried in with groundwater wears impellers, and mineral scale from hard water can foul float switches and check valves. Storm outages are the biggest risk, because a pump that depends on household power stops at the moment the water is rising.

What A Failing Pump Looks Like

Signs Worth Acting On

Runs Without Stopping

Usually a stuck float, a failed check valve, or a pit receiving more water than the pump can move.

Hums But Moves No Water

Often a jammed impeller or a failed start capacitor. Running it like this burns out the motor.

Short Cycling

Rapid on and off cycles point to a bad check valve or a float set in the wrong position.

Grinding Or Rattling

Worn bearings or debris in the impeller housing. Sand is a common cause in this area.

Repair Or Replace

How We Decide

Most residential pumps last roughly seven to ten years. A pump older than that, or one that has already failed twice, is usually cheaper to replace than to keep repairing. A clean repair, such as a new float switch or check valve, makes sense on a newer pump with a sound motor. We tell you which category yours falls into and why.

A battery backup is worth considering for any pump that protects finished space, because it keeps the pit working through the power outages that follow Florida storms. We also check that the discharge line runs to an approved location and is not tied into the sanitary sewer. Installation work follows the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and we tell you up front whether a permit applies to your job.

Before You Call

What To Check First

  • Do not stand in water near the pit or touch a plugged-in pump. If water is rising toward outlets, shut off power at the breaker only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely.
  • Pour a few gallons of water into a sump pit. A healthy pump starts, empties it, and shuts off. Skip this test on a sewage ejector pit.
  • Note the pump's age and brand if the label is readable, and whether it has a battery backup.
FAQs

Common Questions

Do Fort Myers homes even have sump pumps?

Some do, but they are uncommon. With a high water table, slab foundations, and almost no basements, most homes never need one. Pumps turn up in low-lying lots, ground-level enclosures under raised homes, below-grade bathrooms, pool equipment pits, and condo garages.

How do I test my sump pump?

Pour a few gallons of water into the pit. A healthy pump starts, empties the pit, and shuts off. Do not run this test on a sewage ejector pit, and call us if the pump hums, runs on, or does nothing.

Why does my pump run when it has not rained?

High groundwater, a stuck float, a failed check valve that lets water drain back into the pit, or irrigation runoff can all keep a pump cycling. Tidal groundwater near the Caloosahatchee can also raise the pit level between storms.

What is the difference between a sump pump and a sewage ejector pump?

A sump pump moves groundwater or drainage water out of a pit. A sewage ejector pump lifts wastewater from a below-grade bathroom or laundry up to the sewer line. They look similar, but the ejector pit is sealed and needs different handling.

Is a failed sump pump an emergency?

If the pit is overflowing into living space or a garage with stored belongings, yes, call the 24/7 line. If the pump is just noisy or short-cycling, we can usually schedule the repair before the next heavy rain.
