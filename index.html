Why Fort Myers Homes Call Us

Local Plumbing Challenges We Actually See

Tree Root Intrusion

Established oaks and other large trees across Fort Myers send roots searching for moisture inside sewer lines, especially during the dry season. Left unchecked, root intrusion causes recurring clogs that snaking alone will not clear, which is why hydro jetting is often the right long-term fix rather than a repeat visit every few months.

Hard Water From The Local Aquifer

Water drawn from the Southwest Florida limestone aquifer carries mineral content that narrows pipe openings and shortens water heater life over time. If your water heater is failing years earlier than the manufacturer's rated lifespan, hard water scale buildup is a common local cause worth checking before assuming the unit is simply old.

Storm-Season Debris

Heavy rainy-season downpours push sand and debris into older drain and sewer systems, adding stress right when demand for service spikes. Homes on septic systems in particular should have drainfields inspected before hurricane season to reduce the risk of a backup during heavy rain.

Aging Infrastructure In Older Neighborhoods

Many established Fort Myers neighborhoods still run original cast iron or galvanized pipe alongside newer PVC in more recent construction, and older lines fail without much warning. A camera inspection can confirm which pipe material is actually running through an older home before a small leak becomes a larger repair.
