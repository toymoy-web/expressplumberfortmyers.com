Why It's Not One-Size-Fits-All

What Actually Changes Between Areas

A plumbing site that lists ten cities and describes them all the same way isn't giving you useful information. Housing age genuinely predicts pipe material in this region: Fort Myers' historic downtown and North Fort Myers' established riverfront neighborhoods still carry cast iron and galvanized lines that are decades past a typical service life, while Gateway's master-planned subdivisions run modern PVC. Water source matters just as much. Cape Coral and San Carlos Park draw from municipal supply with real, checkable hard-water conditions, while a large share of Lehigh Acres relies on private wells with even more mineral content, and no active sewer expansion reaching most of the town, a genuinely different reality than anywhere else on this list. Soil and drainage add a third variable: Cape Coral's canal system and shifting fill soil behave differently from San Carlos Park's more standard residential lots, and both are quite different again from the storm-surge and tidal groundwater dynamics along North Fort Myers' stretch of the Caloosahatchee. We built a dedicated page for each community specifically so this real variation doesn't get flattened into one generic paragraph that could apply to any Florida city.
