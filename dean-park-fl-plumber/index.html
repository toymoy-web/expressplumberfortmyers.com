About Dean Park

A Historic District East Of Downtown

Dean Park is a historic residential neighborhood about one mile east of downtown Fort Myers. Its streets include Cranford, Evans, First, Michigan, Palm, Providence, and Rhode Island Avenues. The National Register listing describes the district as bounded by 1st Street, Palm Avenue, Michigan Avenue, and Evans Avenue.

The subdivision map was filed on May 1, 1920. The neighborhood was designated a historic district in 1997 and added to the National Register of Historic Places in 2013. The district holds 89 residential structures, and the City's Historic Preservation Commission classifies 62 of them as contributing.

Many of the houses are Craftsman bungalows, with Spanish Revival and Colonial Revival homes mixed in. A century of ownership means most have been remodeled at least once.

Plumbing In A Historic District

What We Check In Older Homes

A house built a hundred years ago has usually had its plumbing updated more than once, so what is inside the walls varies from one address to the next. We inspect before we recommend a repair or a repipe, and we do not assume a particular pipe material from the age of a street.

A camera inspection of a sewer line and leak detection that pinpoints a wet spot both help limit how much of a house has to be opened up. That matters in a home with original plaster walls and period trim.

Because Dean Park is a designated historic district, changes to the outside of a home may involve the City's Historic Preservation Commission. If a job touches the exterior, such as a new water heater vent or an outdoor shutoff, check with the City before work starts.

Plumbing work is governed by the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and many jobs, including water heater replacement and repiping, can require a permit from the building department. Before a repipe or a water heater swap, ask for a written scope that names the pipe material, says whether walls will be opened and who repairs them, and states whether a permit will be pulled.

Ask for a price before work begins. For a drain that runs slowly, avoid chemical drain openers in old lines and call a technician instead. For an active leak, shut off the water at the main valve and call before anyone cuts into a wall, so the repair can be scheduled around the least damage.

Dean Park Plumbing Questions

Where is Dean Park in Fort Myers?

Dean Park is a historic residential district about one mile east of downtown Fort Myers. The National Register listing describes it as bounded by 1st Street, Palm Avenue, Michigan Avenue, and Evans Avenue.

Do you service homes in historic districts?

Service is available for homes of every age, including the historic houses of Dean Park. Before we recommend a repair or a repipe, we inspect what is actually installed.

Do I need City approval for plumbing work on a Dean Park home?

It depends on the job. Interior repairs generally do not change the outside of a house, but exterior changes in a historic district may involve the City's Historic Preservation Commission. Check with the City before work that alters the exterior.

What should I tell you when I call?

Give us your street, the nearest cross street, and what you are seeing, such as a leak, a slow drain, or no hot water. We will confirm your address is covered before scheduling anything.

Is Dean Park inside the City of Fort Myers?

Yes. Dean Park is a historic district within the City of Fort Myers, and the City's Historic Preservation Commission oversees its contributing structures.
