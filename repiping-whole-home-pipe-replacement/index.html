Fixed one leak, and now there's another one somewhere else? When leaks keep showing up at different spots on aging pipe, patching each one individually stops making sense. Repiping replaces the failing pipe material throughout the home, or in the sections affected, instead of chasing one leak at a time as they appear.

Why This Matters In Fort Myers

The Local Cause

Homes built before the 1980s in the Fort Myers area sometimes still run original galvanized steel or early plastic piping that has reached or exceeded its expected lifespan. Hard water from the local limestone aquifer accelerates internal corrosion in older metal pipe, which is a real, checkable factor behind recurring pinhole leaks rather than one-off bad luck. When patch repairs start happening every few months on different sections of pipe, that pattern itself is a strong sign the underlying pipe material, not any one spot, is the actual problem. A whole-home evaluation can usually confirm within a single visit whether the issue is isolated or systemic.

Signs You Need Repiping

When Patching Stops Making Sense

Recurring Pinhole Leaks

Small leaks appearing in different spots over months usually mean the pipe material itself is failing, not isolated bad luck.

Rusty Or Discolored Water

Consistent discoloration, especially from multiple fixtures, often points to internal corrosion throughout the system.

Low Water Pressure Throughout

Corrosion narrowing pipe interiors over time can reduce pressure house-wide, not just at one fixture.

Visible Galvanized Pipe

Older galvanized steel pipe, if visible in a basement or crawlspace, is a strong candidate for planned replacement before it fails.

Before You Call

What To Check First

  • Note how many separate leaks you've had over the past year and roughly where, since a pattern across different areas is the clearest signal repiping is worth discussing.
  • Check for visible galvanized or corroded pipe in a crawlspace, garage, or basement if accessible, and mention it when you call.
  • If water pressure has dropped gradually over months rather than suddenly, mention that timeline, since it helps distinguish a system-wide issue from a single blockage.
FAQs

Common Questions

Do I need to repipe the whole house, or just part of it?

It depends on the pattern. If leaks and corrosion are limited to one section, partial repiping may be enough. Recurring issues across multiple areas usually point toward a full whole-home job.

Will repiping mean tearing up my whole house?

Not necessarily. Many repiping jobs route new pipe through accessible paths like attics and walls with targeted access points, rather than opening every wall in the home.

PEX or copper, which is better?

Both are valid options. PEX is generally less expensive and more resistant to the type of corrosion driven by local hard water; copper has a longer track record and different cost and durability trade-offs we can walk through.

Do you offer free estimates for repiping?

Yes, for non-emergency evaluations. We confirm the price after inspecting the current pipe condition and scope.
