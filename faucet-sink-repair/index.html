Why This Happens Here

The Local Cause

Faucet and sink calls in Fort Myers often trace back to hard water from the local limestone aquifer, which builds mineral scale inside cartridges and valves faster than in areas with softer water. That scale is what usually causes a faucet to drip persistently even after the handle is fully closed, or causes water pressure at a single fixture to drop while the rest of the house is unaffected. Kitchen sink calls also tend to involve worn garbage disposal seals leaking at the sink connection, which is a related but separate issue from the faucet itself. Bathroom sink faucets tend to show scale buildup first, since they're used constantly with the same hard water supply feeding the rest of the home.

Signs You Need Service

Common Problems We Fix

Persistent Dripping

A faucet that drips even when fully closed usually means a worn cartridge or valve seat needs replacement.

Low Water Pressure At One Fixture

If only one faucet has weak pressure, mineral buildup inside that fixture's aerator or cartridge is the likely cause.

Leaking At The Base

Water pooling around the faucet base often means a worn O-ring or failed seal, not the water line itself.

Sink Not Draining Right

Slow draining at a single sink is often local to that fixture's trap or connection, not a whole-house issue.

Before You Call

What To Check First

  • Note whether the drip happens with the faucet fully off or only during use, since that changes which part is likely worn.
  • Check if low pressure affects just one faucet or the whole house. A single-fixture issue is usually local to that faucet.
  • If you're replacing a sink, measure the existing faucet hole spacing, since that determines which new faucets will fit without extra plumbing work.
  • If you're noticing scale buildup on multiple fixtures at once, mention that when you call, since it may point to a household-wide hard water issue rather than one faucet wearing out on its own.
FAQs

Common Questions

Why does my faucet keep dripping after I tighten the handle?

A worn cartridge or valve seat is the most common cause, especially with hard local water accelerating wear. Tightening the handle harder won't fix a worn internal part.

Should I replace or repair an older faucet?

If a faucet is otherwise in good condition, a cartridge or valve replacement is usually the more cost-effective fix. A unit with multiple failing parts or visible corrosion is often better replaced outright.

Can you match my existing faucet finish?

We work with all major brands and finishes and can source a matching or comparable replacement for repairs or full swaps.

Do you offer free estimates?

Yes, for non-emergency work. We confirm the price before any work begins.

Is a leaking sink connection the same as a faucet leak?

Not always. A leak at the connection under the sink can come from the garbage disposal seal or drain trap rather than the faucet itself, which we'll confirm before quoting a repair.
