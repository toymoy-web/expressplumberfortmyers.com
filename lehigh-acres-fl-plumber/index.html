Six Decades Of Growth, One Plumbing City

Lehigh Acres By Housing Era

Lehigh Acres was platted in the 1950s and has grown in layers ever since, which means the plumbing problems here genuinely depend on which decade a given street was built. The original 1960s-70s neighborhoods near the center of town, around Lee Boulevard and Williams Road, run some of the oldest galvanized and early plastic pipe in Lee County. Newer subdivisions breaking ground on the community's expanding southern and western edges, off Gunnery Road and toward the Fort Myers line, use modern PEX and PVC, but sit on the same septic-and-well infrastructure as their older neighbors. Unlike Cape Coral, there's no active sewer expansion program reaching most of Lehigh Acres, so septic isn't a stopgap here, it's the permanent system for the vast majority of homes.

The Septic-And-Well Reality

Why This Isn't Like Coastal Lee County

A large share of Lehigh Acres homes have never had a proper septic inspection or pumping, and it's common to find tanks packed with sludge and drainfields under real stress by the time a backup finally gets our attention. On the water side, a large share of the community draws from private wells rather than treated municipal supply, and mineral-heavy well water here shortens the life of water heaters, fixture cartridges, and valve seats well before their rated lifespan. Heavy rainfall saturates the ground and puts additional pressure on drainage and septic systems already carrying decades of buildup, which is why backups often cluster right after a hard rain.

What To Do Right Now

  • Shut off water at the main valve if you know where it is, then call our 24/7 emergency line.
